# Campaign page quality

Use this reference to turn a user's project evidence into a clear, honest and
feasible crowdfunding page. Ask for proof and source figures. Keep unknowns
visible instead of filling gaps with assumptions.

## 1. Diagnose the project

Establish the project's current reality: concept, prototype, tested offer,
finished product or existing service. Ask what evidence exists, such as supplier
quotes, prototypes, prior sales, pilots, newsletter subscribers, waitlists,
surveys or community engagement. Record evidence precisely and avoid converting
interest into promised sales.

Clarify:

- the project in one sentence;
- the primary audience, its need and its main objection;
- what supporters receive and what is not included;
- the team's relevant experience and current capacity;
- suppliers, production dependencies and logistics assumptions;
- known fixed and variable costs;
- the minimum viable outcome funded by the campaign;
- the three largest delivery risks and possible mitigations;
- permissions, intellectual-property, safety or regulatory checks still needed.

## 2. Define the proposition

Write a simple proposition with four elements: audience, need, solution and
credible difference. Prefer concrete benefits and verifiable proof over slogans.
Separate facts from aspirations. If the project is not final, say whether an
image or specification represents a concept, prototype or production version.

Create a short pitch of three or four sentences answering: what it is, for whom,
why it matters now and why this team can deliver it.

## 3. Calculate a conservative goal

Never guess a funding goal and never invent Ulule fees. Ask the user for current
quotes, a bill of materials and applicable tax or fee information. Ask them to
verify current Ulule rules and fees in their account or current documentation.

Use this generic model with verified inputs:

```text
Funding goal = fixed costs
             + variable fulfilment costs at the planned quantity
             + packaging and delivery costs not charged separately
             + applicable platform/payment fees
             + applicable taxes and duties
             + justified contingency
             - secured financing that genuinely reduces the need
```

Distinguish fixed costs from per-unit costs. Test at least the expected reward
mix and a less favourable mix. For each reward, verify:

```text
reward price >= unit production cost
              + packaging and handling
              + delivery subsidy, if any
              + allocated fixed costs
              + applicable fees and taxes
              + contingency
```

Do not prescribe a universal contingency percentage. Ask the user to justify a
buffer based on supplier reliability, lead times, currency exposure, waste and
technical uncertainty. If figures are missing, provide a calculation template,
not a fabricated total.

## 4. Structure the page

Prepare the description in this order:

1. **Headline and subheadline:** one clear promise and differentiator.
2. **Short pitch:** what, for whom, why now and current project stage.
3. **Problem and solution:** the need, the proposed response and concrete use.
4. **Evidence:** prototype, tests, prior work, partners or traction that can be
   substantiated.
5. **What supporters receive:** content, specifications, variants, limitations
   and exclusions.
6. **Use of funds:** transparent budget categories backed by real figures.
7. **Production and fulfilment:** suppliers, manufacturing, quality control,
   packaging, delivery and dependencies.
8. **Milestones:** campaign, production, testing and delivery windows with
   buffers.
9. **Rewards:** simple comparison of price, contents, limits and estimated
   delivery.
10. **Team:** relevant experience, responsibilities and capacity.
11. **Risks and mitigations:** specific, material and understandable.
12. **Impact and responsibility:** only substantiated environmental or social
    claims; include trade-offs where relevant.
13. **FAQ:** shipping, customs, variants, sizing, compatibility and current
    cancellation or refund rules to verify.

Use short paragraphs, descriptive headings and direct language. Remove repeated
claims and unsupported superlatives.

## 5. Prepare honest visuals

Build a visual brief before requesting or uploading files:

- a legible hero image showing the project clearly;
- real product or prototype photographs from several angles;
- an in-use image with an honest sense of scale;
- diagrams for dimensions, features or process when useful;
- team and behind-the-scenes images that support credibility;
- a short caption for every image and accessible alt text;
- subtitles or captions for video.

Never present a render, mock-up or AI-generated image as a finished product.
Label concepts and prototypes. Do not fabricate people, testimonials, partners,
certifications, performance results or environmental benefits. Check that the
user holds the necessary rights to every asset.

## 6. Design viable rewards or presales

Keep the choice understandable. A useful architecture often includes an entry
option, one obvious core offer and a small number of premium or limited offers.
Use bundles only when they improve value without making fulfilment fragile.

For each reward capture:

- title and exact contents;
- price and verified unit economics;
- included and excluded delivery costs;
- quantity cap based on production capacity;
- variants and how choices will be collected;
- shipping zones and customs assumptions;
- estimated delivery month and year with buffer;
- any age, safety, availability or legal constraint.

Avoid tiny price differences, unlimited complex variants, rewards whose labour
cannot scale and stretch goals that destroy margin or delay the core promise.
Ask the user to verify current rules for cancellations, refunds, exchanges,
shipping and restricted goods.

## 7. Build a realistic calendar

Map the critical path: final design, supplier order, tooling, certifications,
manufacturing, quality control, packaging and shipping. Show dependencies and
add buffers where a delay would affect delivery.

Also prepare the communication plan: pre-launch audience activation, launch
communications, mid-campaign updates, final reminders and a transparent update
cadence during production. Do not represent this plan as a guarantee of funding.

## 8. Disclose risks and impact

Cover the risks that could materially affect supporters: single-source parts,
minimum order quantities, manufacturing yield, certification, customs, carrier
delays, currency movements, unexpected costs and team capacity. Pair each risk
with a credible mitigation or state that it remains unresolved.

Use measured impact language. Ask for evidence behind environmental and social
claims. Do not use generic environmental claims such as “green” or “eco-friendly”
or offset-based carbon-neutral claims. Mention materials, sourcing,
repairability, waste and end-of-life only when relevant, specific and
supportable.

## 9. Validate before handoff

Confirm with the user that:

- all financial inputs come from real sources and current rules;
- every reward has non-negative, resilient unit economics;
- production capacity matches reward caps;
- delivery zones, costs, customs and windows are explicit;
- variants can be collected and fulfilled reliably;
- claims, testimonials, partners and certifications are verifiable;
- concepts and prototypes are labelled honestly;
- the page includes the proposition, evidence, budget, plan, team, risks and FAQ;
- legal, rights and safety questions have been checked by the appropriate person;
- all remaining unknowns are listed;
- the user understands that the MCP Public Ulule prepares an unpublished draft
  and that the campaign is never published automatically.

## Common failures

Do not invent costs or fees, price before calculating fulfilment, hide shipping
complexity, promise an unbuffered date, create too many tiers, use misleading
visuals, omit risks, claim guaranteed success or leave no plan for post-campaign
communication.
